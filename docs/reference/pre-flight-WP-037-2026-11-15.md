## After execution — lessons learned

**Lesson: read the move and the snap in the same tick.** The snap tests where the piece was before this tick's move, so it comes a tick after the mouse brings the piece close. And a piece carried exactly onto its place never snaps: the move is skipped when it is there, and the next click sends it back to its start. The decompile hid both; the disassembly showed them.

**Lesson: drive a drag through the engine's own input.** The check moves the mouse with the engine's hover and click path, one tick at a time, so the piece numbering, the hit test and the cursor are the ones a player gets. The same points then drove the live run.

**Lesson: a class holding several rooms needs their names.** Nine rooms share the class, and each keeps its keys in its own section, so the factory hands each instance its room's name.

**Scope note:** one fork commit, 5 files staged by name. The class went in `chase_rooms` with the close-up, and its check in the chase check. The shared files got one hook (`hidesCursor`), told to the other session.
